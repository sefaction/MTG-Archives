# Public issue intake and joint review

Public submission needs a title and one description in the reporter's own words. The optional details box can hold reproduction, expected behavior, screenshots or environment. Users do not need to understand labels, roadmap hierarchy, work done/remaining or acceptance criteria. The general form is the fallback when they are unsure. Maintainers gather any missing context through focused conversation.

[AGENTS.md](../AGENTS.md) is the canonical policy. Honor explicit user priorities and urgent security/data-integrity fixes, then finish the oldest agreed human feature requests and bugs, respecting dependencies. An unfinished coherent batch takes precedence over starting another audit or qualification ticket. Pending clarification blocks that decision, not independent authorized work.

## Origin is separate from readiness

Brian and ChatGPT may use the same GitHub account. An author login, bot identity, polished prose, labels (including `codex`) or an issue-body declaration cannot establish whether ChatGPT filed an issue. Do not exclude Brian's hand-written issues from joint review because they look like internal work or have been assigned to a worker.

For a newly agent-created issue, the creating worker must retain an explicit creation receipt in its worker-controlled checkpoint/journal as part of the authorized creation operation. Record repository, returned issue node ID and number, creation timestamp, worker run/thread identifier and task/authorization reference. Preserve the creation operation's returned identity/readback, rather than manufacturing a receipt later from the issue's current body or labels. Receipts contain no credentials. An optional visible origin note can link the worker/run evidence, but the note alone is not verification.

A reviewer correlates the receipt with the issue's immutable identity and creation metadata and the named trusted worker record. Missing, inaccessible, mismatched or conflicting evidence means **unknown origin**, not agent-origin. This batch defines that protocol; it does not backfill old receipts, add a provenance service or claim the recurring reviewer already implements receipt verification. Existing historical records may be used only when they establish creation, not merely later work on the issue. Human and unknown-origin requests share the joint review path. Enriching a human issue never changes its original human origin.

`triage: complete` is an explicit readiness opt-in for internal structural validation, not provenance or permission to implement. A type/area/status label for sorting, or `codex` for worker assignment, must not trigger the checklist before joint review. Newly agent-created implementation issues must satisfy full internal standards and carry the readiness label from creation within their existing authorized scope, with their creation receipt. A shared-login issue lacking both a receipt and readiness marker cannot safely be identified as agent-created; workers must obey the creation protocol. That limitation must not turn ordinary human submission back into an internal checklist.

## Review before enrichment

1. The recurring reviewer reads new or meaningfully changed human/unknown-origin issues and existing discussion/decision records. Include Brian's hand-written reports even when they already have internal labels. Do not skip solely because `sefaction`, a bot, `codex` or readiness labels appear. A verified agent-origin issue follows its existing authorized scope instead; it receives no new authority from that classification.
2. Discuss intent with Brian first. Offer a short interpretation and ask only unresolved questions needed to agree the intended outcome, uncertainty, boundaries or consequential choices; do not ask him to complete a template. Reuse recorded discussion/decisions, including a clear direct request that already settles intent, instead of repeating questions. Keep an unresolved decision pending review and continue independent authorized work.
3. Once intent is agreed, re-read the latest issue/comments and add a concise current summary: agreed problem/outcome; evidence and limits; delivered work; exact remaining step; acceptance/completion criteria; and relevant parent/dependency/PR links. Scale detail to the work; state when a category is not applicable instead of inventing requirements or evidence. Record the actual discussion/decision reference, preserve the original report/history and defer conflicting concurrent edits.
4. Apply correct internal type/status/area dimensions and scope flags, without guessing a blocked choice. Then explicitly add `triage: complete` to activate internal checks; read back edits. Preserve original human/unknown origin. The implementation worker can act only within agreed scope; intent review is not merge/deployment approval. Removing the readiness label returns validation to intake; it does not itself complete work, prove consent or erase provenance.
5. Save reviewed issue identity, meaningful revision and decision reference in the reviewer checkpoint to avoid repeatedly asking about unchanged reports. Substantive changes or missing decisions return to joint review. Structural validation alone cannot establish this decision gate.

## Internal tracking and follow-up

Before opening or enriching an issue, search open and closed issues and relevant PRs for duplicates, read current parent scope/latest comments and reconcile merged work with what remains. Keep useful bug creation: a distinct actionable defect should record reproduction or uncertainty, impact, expected behavior and its resolving PR. Incidental findings, failed harness attempts and evidence gaps normally belong in the existing issue/PR; create another issue only when independently actionable remaining work needs separate follow-up.

Use these dimensions after triage; they are maintainer responsibilities, not submission requirements:

- **Work type (exactly one):** `type: bug` (broken behavior), `type: feature` (new capability), `type: improvement` (better existing behavior), `type: maintenance` (upkeep), or `type: validation` (acceptance/evidence work).
- **Workflow status (exactly one):** `status: backlog` (available, unclaimed work), `status: in progress` (implementation/qualification underway, or an actively progressing tracker), `status: investigating` (cause not established), `status: awaiting review` (applicable qualification complete and PR ready for individual review), `status: awaiting validation` (specific acceptance evidence pending), or `status: needs decision` (a consequential choice blocks the next action). A draft with unfinished applicable qualification is not awaiting review. Labels do not replace exact-owner locks or claim comments; preserve the existing `in-progress` claim label where used.
- **Area (one or more):** `area: recognition`, `area: scanner`, `area: review`, `area: inventory`, `area: data integrity`, `area: infrastructure`, `area: test infrastructure`. Add a similarly named area only for a distinct domain that existing areas cannot express.
- **Scope flags:** `roadmap` marks a coordinated top-level product roadmap; `tracker` marks an umbrella/phase issue linking narrower work and acceptance. The top roadmap may carry both. Neither means deferred, and a bug tracker remains `type: bug`.
- **Planning flag:** `deferred` marks intentionally postponed scope. Keep its type, area and workflow status; a deferred bug remains a bug. Record why, its parent and the reconsideration trigger. Do not defer a whole active tracker because some children are future work.

`type: feature` replaces the overloaded `enhancement` label for this queue; `deferred` replaces future-only use of `roadmap`. Do not globally rename/delete legacy labels or remove unrelated labels, assignees or other owners' claims. A GitHub Projects board is not required.

Keep delivered, remaining and deferred scope explicit so completed work is not reimplemented. Preserve older scope as dated history. Investigations require one bounded next test, the evidence it must capture and a stopping criterion; a non-reproducing rerun leaves the historical cause unknown. Audits remain useful when requested, when a concrete risk warrants them, or when no eligible agreed work remains; they should lead to product improvements rather than an expanding checklist queue.

Each PR identifies affected issues/parents and uses the [PR checklist](../.github/pull_request_template.md). After a merge authorized under AGENTS.md, the merger re-reads those issues/latest comments, updates current summaries with merged PR/revision and evidence limits, reconciles remaining/deferred scope and labels, then reads back edits. Preserve original reports, decisions and failed evidence; never overwrite a concurrent edit. Close only completed scope or a visible transfer of every remainder with criteria and rationale; link resolving PRs and remove open-workflow status labels on closure. Distinguish author evidence, independent review, exact-head CI, merge, named-installation deployment and user acceptance. A partial merge does not close a broad issue or establish deployment/acceptance.

Useful saved filters:

- [Current work](https://github.com/sefaction/MTG-Archives/issues?q=is%3Aissue%20is%3Aopen%20-label%3Adeferred%20-label%3Atracker): non-deferred individual work, including investigations, decisions and review/validation waits; filter for eligibility and order by original issue creation time under AGENTS.md's priorities.
- [Roadmap and acceptance](https://github.com/sefaction/MTG-Archives/issues?q=is%3Aissue%20is%3Aopen%20label%3Atracker%20-label%3Adeferred): current roadmap, phases and broad acceptance trackers; #302 is the top roadmap.
- [Future work](https://github.com/sefaction/MTG-Archives/issues?q=is%3Aissue%20is%3Aopen%20label%3Adeferred): intentionally postponed bugs, features, improvements and validation.
